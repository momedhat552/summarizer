The Great Barrier Reef is the largest coral reef system in the world, stretching over 2,300 kilometres along the coast of Queensland, Australia. It is made up of nearly 3,000 individual reefs and about 900 islands, and it can be seen from space.

The reef is home to an enormous variety of life, including more than 1,500 species of fish, 400 types of coral, and six of the world's seven species of marine turtle. Many of these animals depend on the reef for food and shelter.

In recent decades the reef has been damaged by rising ocean temperatures, which cause coral bleaching, as well as by pollution and storms. Scientists and conservation groups are working on ways to protect it, including reducing runoff from farms and testing heat-resistant corals.
